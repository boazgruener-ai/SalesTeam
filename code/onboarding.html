SalesTeam Setup

Tell us who you're looking for. This scopes every future Target Account & Contact Discovery scan - you can change any of it later from Settings' "Change Settings".

Pick the setting you want to change, then press Save. Nothing is changed until you do.

How SalesTeam works with online platforms and data sources. SalesTeam is built with the rules of the online platforms and data sources it works with in mind. Its research draws on publicly available sources: the research prompt has an AI assistant consult public company registries and websites, and SalesTeam's own scans read pages of platforms such as LinkedIn. It is designed for personal use, the way a person would use these sites: every scan starts when you click a button, and automatic account preparation runs only if you switch it on: you start it once; SalesTeam keeps your accounts ready within your daily LinkedIn limit, visible in your browser. Every search and page visit happens in a browser tab or window you can see, only while Chrome is open, and you can turn the automation off at any time in Settings. There is no bulk collection of pages. A built-in safety limit keeps the number of searches and page visits per day modest - within what one person could do by hand. SalesTeam has no server or database of its own, so it never collects, resells or shares anyone's data: everything it finds stays on your device, for your own use, and it never sends messages or connection requests for you. Because each platform's terms of use apply to how any tool is used on it (LinkedIn's terms, for example), we recommend keeping your use moderate and within the daily limit.

About you

Who you are and which company you sell for. With your company's website, SalesTeam can research it and propose answers for the next steps - you check, change or clear each one before moving on.

All research runs on your own Anthropic API key, which stays in this browser. You can also add it later in Settings - every step works without it.

Researching your company…

    Where are your target companies and leads (posts)?

    This is your one target geography - it scopes both which companies Discovery searches for and which of your already-scanned leads (Posts/Jobs) get kept. Discovery's own company search can only actually query LinkedIn for countries with a live-verified location code (more are added over time) - anything you pick beyond that still applies to filtering leads, just not to the search itself yet.

    Double-click a country to move it, or select one or more (Ctrl/Shift-click) and use the arrows.

    Choose at least one continent or country.

    Location priority

    How much should each location count when we prioritize what we find? Defaults to Medium - only touch the ones you want to push up or down. Shown per continent if you picked more than one continent above, per country otherwise.

    What size companies?

    Check the size ranges you want to include, each with its own priority. How many companies to find is set in the last step, "How big is your list".

    Any specific industries? (optional)

    Leave unchecked for no industry restriction. Only industries with a live-verified LinkedIn code are offered below (same reasoning as Location) - more are added over time; LinkedIn's own catalog has 200+ entries in total. Each checked industry also gets its own priority for scoring, defaulting to Medium.

    Organization types other than companies (optional)

    Companies - private or publicly traded - are always included, so they are not in this list. It is only about the other kinds of organization: non-profits, public administrations, universities and the like. Such an organization isn't automatically a bad fit - it can be a perfectly good buyer depending on its size and budget. Choose per type: Yes (treat it like a company, the default), No (leave it out), or Review (keep it, but flag it for you to decide yourself). Hover over a type to see what it covers.

    A discovered organization is caught only when its LinkedIn card shows one of these type names - one filed here but showing a more specific label (e.g. a non-profit whose card says "International Affairs") is caught only after a visit to its own page, which is not spent automatically. Only types with a live-verified LinkedIn code are offered - more are added over time.

    Languages to consider

    Which languages should we consider when building search terms (e.g. a country's own local name)? English is always included.

    Discovery Prioritization: how should we rank the companies we find?

    Rank the three criteria by overall importance. Which specific locations/sizes/industries matter most is set per-item on their own steps (Location/Size/Industry) - this just orders the three criteria against each other. Feeds automatic company scoring once Discovery scanning ships. This governs Discovery's own company search - for the separate rules that already prioritize scanned Topic leads (posts) today, see the next step, Leads Prioritization.

    Each rank must be a different criterion.

    Leads Prioritization: how should we rank the leads (posts) your scans find?

    After every scan the Sales Mentor ranks each new lead (post) from P1 (best) to P5. The rules on this page adjust that ranking when the lead's company is one of your Target Accounts. (Ranking the companies themselves is a different step: Discovery Prioritization.) The Sales Mentor's own judgment always comes first; a rule only changes the ranking of a lead that meets its condition.

    Posts

    A post is written by a real, named person you can contact - the stronger signal. Each post is checked against the rules below, top to bottom; the first enabled rule whose conditions all match applies its effect (a floor, a ceiling, or a fixed priority). If no rule matches, the Sales Mentor ranks the post on its own. Order matters, so put more specific rules above more general ones with ↑/↓.

    Post rules

    Build a rule from any of: a column of your Target Accounts (type a column name; the box suggests the ones your list has), "Account is in the Target Accounts list", "Post author is a Target Contact", "Post matched a Topic".

    Job ads

    A job ad comes from the company itself - there is no person to contact yet, so it is a weaker signal than a post. Job ads have two fixed rules instead of rules you build: a job ad tells SalesTeam little more than the company and the job title, so there is nothing else to build a rule from. You can switch either rule off or change its priority.

    An account's level is its research workbook rating when it has one, otherwise SalesTeam's own Priority: P1 = Very High, P2 = High, P3 = Medium, P4-P5 = Low.

    Which initiatives matter to you?

    SalesTeam looks for each account's recent initiatives and projects - a reason to get in touch. Tick the stages where what you sell helps, and put the most important first with the arrows. Initiatives at an unticked stage are left out, and only the ticked ones count towards your initiatives target.

    Any companies you want in your list for sure? (optional)

    You do not need to list your target customers here: SalesTeam researches and builds your list of target companies itself, from the criteria you set in this setup. Use this step only for companies you want in the list whether or not the research finds them - for example a company you are already talking to, or one just outside your size or industry criteria. Leave it empty if there are none.

    SalesTeam adds these first, whatever their size or industry, and researches them like every other account. One company per line: its name, and its website after a comma if you know it (for example Acme AG, https://www.acme.ch).

    Any companies to exclude? (optional)

    A company on these lists never shows up in your Target Accounts and is never treated as a lead (post). Add each company by its name, its LinkedIn company page or its website - a LinkedIn page is the surest match. Customers and partners are not excluded: they have their own step next.

    Customers and partners (optional)

    Your existing customers and partners stay normal accounts - researched, their contacts found and their leads kept - with a Customer or Partner tag. A customer's priority is raised one level, and the AI is told it is not a cold prospect. A company can be both.

    Any company aliases to map? (optional)

    Some LinkedIn company pages aren't independently relevant buying organizations - they're a brand, program, or local-market name run by a real company (e.g. "Audi Schweiz" is really AMAG). Map those here so a discovered page like that is attributed to its real, canonical company instead of becoming a duplicate account. One pair per line: the alias's LinkedIn company URL, then ->, then the canonical company's LinkedIn URL.

    How big should your list be?

    SalesTeam builds your account list up to these numbers and then stops: it finds new accounts on the web, researches them, and checks them on LinkedIn within your daily limit. You can raise the numbers later; lowering them never removes an account.

    How to handle research findings

    When a web research turns up a fact that disagrees with what an account already holds, most of the time it is not a mistake: two correct figures counted differently (84,146 employees or 150,000 – direct staff against staff-and-contractors), or a field that is never used to score a company at all. Resolve Web Findings Automatically… on the Target Accounts Dashboard settles those by the rules below and leaves you only the ones that can genuinely change something. It always shows you what it will do before it does it, writes every single decision to the Activity Log, and can be undone in one action.

    Rules

    Turning a rule off always moves in the safe direction. For the rules that decide something, that case stops being decided and comes to you instead. For the rules that always ask, it stops forcing a question and lets the remaining rules try – but a value that fails the “looks wrong” checks is never written into an account whatever these are set to, so the most a switched-off rule can do is keep what you already have.

    Settings the rules use

    Only used when the two counts fall in different size bands. Higher means fewer questions and more decided for you.

    For every number an import brings (employees, revenue). Larger differences go through the same rules as web findings; only those that could change a priority come to Decisions, and in a team only to the Team Lead.

    Keeping current data is the safer default: it is the curated set and it is consistent across every account. Choose web findings instead if the imported data is older or less trustworthy than a fresh search.

    A count fetched from LinkedIn is the size band the company publishes about itself. It is not an exact headcount, but it is the most reliable thing available for putting a company in the right band – so when it disagrees with the web research about the band, this keeps LinkedIn's rather than asking you.

    What counts as a value that “looks wrong”

    Checked on both sides – the found value and the one already stored. Alongside these numbers, rule 2 also catches an employee count that is not a number at all (values like “6,500+” are stored on some accounts and can never be used for sizing), a local employee count larger than the worldwide one, any negative value, and revenue that looks like it was entered in millions where the other side is in units.

    Revenue & Currency

    Revenue reaches SalesTeam written every possible way – 102M, CHF 102m, 102'000, 1.2bn, sometimes just USD with no number at all. It is cleaned up into plain digits (102M becomes 102,000,000) and anything not already in the currency below is converted into it, so two figures can actually be compared instead of merely looking different. The same clean-up rescues employee counts stored as text, like 6,500+, which otherwise give a company no size at all when it is scored. The tables, the account pages and every export show revenue in this currency; each figure is also kept as it was found, so choosing another currency later loses nothing.

    Exchange rates

    These are approximate starting values, not a live feed – SalesTeam never calls out to a rates service. Correct any of them by hand and set the date so you can see at a glance how old they are. Each one is how many US dollars one unit of that currency is worth.
