How SalesTeam works with online platforms and data sources. SalesTeam is built with the rules of the online platforms and data sources it works with in mind. Its research draws on publicly available sources: the research prompt has an AI assistant consult public company registries and websites, and SalesTeam's own scans read pages of platforms such as LinkedIn. It is designed for personal use, the way a person would use these sites: every scan starts when you click a button, every search and page visit happens in a browser tab you can see, and your browser has to stay open while it runs. There are no scheduled scans and no bulk collection of pages. A built-in safety limit keeps the number of searches and page visits per day modest - within what one person could do by hand. SalesTeam has no server or database of its own, so it never collects, resells or shares anyone's data: everything it finds stays on your device, for your own use, and it never sends messages or connection requests for you. Because each platform's terms of use apply to how any tool is used on it (LinkedIn's terms, for example), we recommend keeping your use moderate and within the daily limit.

Organization types (optional)

A nonprofit or government body isn't automatically disqualifying - it can be a completely legitimate buyer depending on its size and budget, or a bad fit purely because it's too small, not because of its type. Choose per type: Yes (treat as a normal candidate, the default), No (exclude wholesale), or Review (keep it, but flag it for you to look at yourself rather than deciding automatically). Only catches a discovered company when its own LinkedIn card literally displays one of these type names - a company categorized here but displaying a more specific label (e.g. a non-profit whose card says "International Affairs") won't be caught without visiting its own page, a real cost this doesn't spend automatically. Only types with a live-verified LinkedIn code are offered below - more are added over time.

Leads Prioritization: how should we rank the leads (posts) we already scan?

These layer on top of the Sales Mentor's own judgment for a lead whose company is in your Target Accounts list (Target Accounts Dashboard) - not the Discovery step above, which governs company search instead. The Sales Mentor's own judgment is always the base decision and can never itself be disabled; these only ever constrain or override it for a lead that satisfies their specific condition. Saved immediately as you change them, same as everywhere else in this wizard.

A company is treated as confidently matched once it's labeled "Very High" or "High" by your research (never Provisional or lower, whatever its score) and its score is at or above this number. Doesn't affect the Target Accounts Dashboard's own Import/Export - every company there is included regardless of this threshold. Only governs the Job rules below - the Post rules further down set their own thresholds, one per rule.

Target Account confidence threshold must be a number from 0 to 100.

Post rules

Post leads (a real, named individual - a stronger signal than a Job listing) are checked against the rules below, top to bottom. Each rule is a set of conditions that all have to match (AND) - build one from a Workbook column check, "Account is in the Target Accounts list", "Post author is a Target Contact" and/or "Post matched a Topic". The first enabled rule whose conditions all match wins and applies its Floor/Ceiling/Decisive effect; if no rule matches, the Sales Mentor scores the lead entirely on its own. Order matters - only the first match is used, so reorder with ↑/↓ to put more specific rules above more general ones. A Workbook column condition's column field suggests every column actually found in your imported Target Accounts workbook's Companies sheet as you type - or type any column name directly, even one not currently suggested.

What do you sell?

Describe your company and what you actually offer. Shared with AI drafting, the Sales Mentor, and Customer Voice.

What can you offer as a value-add? (optional)

Real resources only, one per line — an article link, a report, "free 20-min demo". The AI will only ever mention something from this list when drafting, never invent one.

Who's your ideal customer?

Company size, geography, what they're investing in - independent of what you offer.

Who should we look for at each company?

Exact job titles, one per line, and/or broader title keywords, one per line. At least one of the two is required - this is who a future contact-discovery scan searches each company for.

LinkedIn has no seniority filter on a company's People tab, so each checked level just adds its own keywords (e.g. VP-level adds "VP", "Vice President", "SVP"...) to the title search above - it never replaces your exact titles or title keywords. The priority you set for each level also lets Post-lead prioritization rules require a minimum seniority.

When a company has more qualifying people than this, exact-title matches are kept ahead of keyword-only matches, not just whichever appeared first.

Enter at least one exact title or title keyword.

Any companies to exclude? (optional)

A company here is never treated as a lead, regardless of anything else about it. One LinkedIn company-page URL per line in each box below - not a company name, since names are too easy to mismatch (legal suffixes, short vs. long names, misspellings). Each line is checked as you go.

Pre-filled with well-known global agencies to save you the lookup; add your local ones and remove any that don't apply, or clear the list entirely if you'd rather skip this.

Anything else that doesn't fit the categories above.

Any company aliases to map? (optional)

Some LinkedIn company pages aren't independently relevant buying organizations - they're a brand, program, or local-market name run by a real company (e.g. "Audi Schweiz" is really AMAG). Map those here so a discovered page like that is attributed to its real, canonical company instead of becoming a duplicate account. One pair per line: the alias's LinkedIn company URL, then ->, then the canonical company's LinkedIn URL.
